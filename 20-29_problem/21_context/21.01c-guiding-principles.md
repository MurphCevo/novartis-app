# Guiding Principles

> Back to [Conceptual Integrity](21.01-conceptual-integrity.md)

---

These principles govern design trade-offs across the project. When two good ideas conflict, these decide which wins.

---

## 1. Human-in-the-loop, always

AI handles coordination and drafting. Humans direct, refine, and approve. No content reaches a customer without a human decision. This is non-negotiable in a regulated pharma context.

---

## 2. Compliance by design, not by gate

Don't wait until step 5 of 7 to check compliance. Embed compliance rules, reference linking, and governance filters into the workflow at every stage. Shift validation left so rework cycles shrink.

---

## 3. Connect, don't replace

The solution sits alongside existing tools  - not in place of them. Marketers keep working in their current systems. Autopilot connects the gaps between those systems. No rip-and-replace.

---

## 4. Prove before scaling

Every capability starts as a proof-of-concept with measurable success criteria. We don't build the full workbench until agents have demonstrated trust, quality, and utility in constrained pilots.

---

## 5. Data readiness is a precondition, not our problem to solve

We build agents that consume clean APIs and structured data. If a source system's data isn't ready, that's surfaced early and pushed back to the client to resolve. We don't absorb data integration debt.

---

## 6. Ownership transfers to the client

Architecture, knowledge, and operational control must be transferable. No black boxes. No vendor lock-in to us. The client's internal teams should be able to maintain, extend, and operate what we build.

---

## 7. Quality over speed-to-demo

A demo that works on happy-path inputs but fails under real conditions is worthless in a compliance environment. We optimise for reliability, accuracy, and auditability  - not for impressive first impressions.

---

## Using These Principles

When making a design decision, ask:

- Does this keep a human in the approval loop?
- Does this embed compliance earlier or push it later?
- Does this connect to existing tools or create a new silo?
- Can we prove this works before committing to scale?
- Are we assuming clean data we haven't validated?
- Could the client run this without us?
- Would this hold up under real-world edge cases?

If the answer to any of these is uncomfortable, raise it in `33_decisions/`.
